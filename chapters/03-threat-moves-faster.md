# Chapter 3 — The Threat Moves Faster Than the Operator Can Respond

## Chapter job
Examine threat velocity, decision latency, and response readiness under service-continuity constraints. Chapter 2 maps *where exposure exists*; this chapter tests whether the operator can *decide and act in time*.

## Argument spine
1. Threats can evolve faster than human-paced incident processes and cross-team handoffs.
2. In a high-availability network, containment decisions can themselves disrupt customers or essential services.
3. Distributed authority across security, network, operations, suppliers, and business leaders can delay action.
4. AI-enabled defense can accelerate response, but unclear authority and guardrails can amplify mistakes.

## Evidence and examples to develop
- Use recent, sourced evidence for threat acceleration; do not infer that every incident is AI-enabled.
- Build an operator-specific tabletop scenario where rapid containment conflicts with continuity obligations.
- Identify the decision rights and escalation points that determine response time.

## Reader decision
Which high-impact incident decisions must be made faster, by whom, and with what safety constraints?
