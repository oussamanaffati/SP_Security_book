## 2. Primary-source contradictions and unresolved stages

1. **Heavy Reading field dates:** Directly re-downloaded the original October 2025 PDF from the RADCOM-hosted report URL. Printed p. 3 says the questionnaire was “fielded globally … in July/August 2025.” Figure 11 (printed p. 16) and Figure 19 (printed p. 25) each state “Source: Heavy Reading (now part of Omdia), June 2025.” The report does not say whether June denotes a distinct figure-specific collection period, source/cut date, or something else, and does not reconcile the notes with the stated fieldwork. Keep unresolved; do not infer a separate sample, editorial error, or date hierarchy.
2. **ENISA 188-report scope and geography:** Directly downloaded the current PDF linked from ENISA’s publication page (published 15 July 2025). Printed p. 7 says EU providers report significant continuity-impact incidents to NRAs and NRAs send annual summaries to ENISA using agreed EU-wide thresholds; it says the document aggregates reports received in 2024. Printed p. 3 says 188 reports came from 26 EU Member States plus two EFTA countries; printed p. 11 says 25 EU Member States plus two EFTA countries reported the 188 incidents. The current official publication page repeats 26 and showed no correction notice. Neither the PDF nor the accessible publication page resolves the discrepancy; omit the country count outside the ledger/register. The 188 are authority-submitted significant/major incident reports, not 188 cyberattacks, and include outages, system failures, human errors, natural phenomena and malicious actions. The separate 30 figure is explicitly sourced to ENISA’s OSINT collection (original overview, printed p. 3); its p. 4 lists selected affected-country abbreviations but does not define complete geographic coverage or a census denominator.
3. **Deutsche Telekom RAN Guardian timeline:** the February 2026 announcement says “since launch in November 2025”; the October 2026 transcript says live in Germany “since January.” These may represent different rollout milestones, but the sources do not explain them. Preserve each date with source attribution.
4. **Deutsche Telekom MINDR:** on 5 October 2026 the transcript says the MVP was “about to go live” and the first MVP use case would be live “by the end of the year.” No later source was verified in this audit; production status after 5 October remains open. The live/demo content is not production-result evidence.
5. **Google Cloud/One NZ VoLTE agent:** one vendor page says One NZ “has deployed” the agent into operations and quotes its executive saying One NZ “is deploying” it. The source does not resolve whether this means production, rollout, or a planned deployment; there is no operator metric or scope.
6. **Check Point/Protiviti ranking:** the directly retrieved Check Point post body (via its official WordPress REST API) says telecom had the highest YoY percentage increase (94%) but was third by weekly volume (2,664). Protiviti calls it a survey and says third-highest percentage increase. The direct primary post resolves the wording/rank conflict; the telecom denominator, event definition and sampling method remain undisclosed.
7. **China Mobile claims are not interchangeable:** TM Forum repeats >30% backend O&M manpower and 30% MTTR claims without a linked operator source; the directly read GSMA page calls its 83%/5× result a pilot; ZTE’s release reports 30% fewer monitoring personnel at one event and 20% traffic growth at another. Different tasks, source types and denominators; no shared baseline or causal comparison. Exclude the percentages from headline outcomes.
8. **Orange Sharlie:** Orange’s March 2026 release describes the service as launched/available but says its >3-million annual conversation capacity is “once deployed.” Treat launch status and prospective capacity separately; do not report capacity as actual traffic.

## 3. Missing denominators, methods, and local conditions

- **Check Point:** telecom-only protected-organization count, exact attack/event definition, telemetry selection, deduplication, observation window and whether the weekly figure is a mean/median are unknown to this audit.
- **Heavy Reading:** 84 people do not equal 84 operators; item n values vary (83; 82–83); the June versus July/August date discrepancy is unresolved; self-assessed autonomy and intentions are not observed production or control effectiveness.
- **Deutsche Telekom:** action/event sample, error and override rates, rollback, time measurement, service impact and start-date milestone are not public in the reviewed announcement. MINDR’s production status needs a dated post-October operator statement.
- **One NZ:** the 80% analysis-time claim lacks case count, baseline, timing definition, answer quality, rework/override and cost. The generator-placement assistant is a separate workflow and should not be described as network-state control.
- **Orange:** MAIA’s deployment/volume are operator-reported, but there is no public conversation-quality, error, advisor-acceptance, cost or service-outcome measure. Sharlie’s annual capacity is not realized usage.
- **Telefónica:** no public inventory or denominator for 500+ “AI use cases,” definition of “in production,” or evidence behind 15 Level 4 cases; NOA’s production use and outcomes are unstated.
- **Airtel:** case metrics lack measurement window, baseline, definitions and denominator; “26% customer experience improvement” is undefined.
- **China Mobile/ZTE:** event-specific staffing/traffic metrics lack headcount, counterfactual, observation window and independently validated operational outcome. GSMA 83%/5× and TM Forum 30%/30% remain separate unverified claims. ZTE’s 2026 white paper directly makes a vendor claim of >10 sites and reports GP Bangladesh MTTR down 12.5%, human-machine collaboration efficiency up 70%, and outstanding fault work orders down 3%; site allocation, stage, baseline, period, denominators and KPI definitions are absent.
- **TELUS:** the primary and company-distributed release bodies remain unreadable; omit the numbers. **BT:** the release body confirms aggregate call counts, but not the call-attempt denominator, accuracy, false positives or prevented fraud losses; use as product/service telemetry only, not efficacy.
- **TM Forum interim ROI:** 78 validated operator respondents overall, but question-level n, geography, start date, sampling frame, weighting and “can easily link” definition are not given; figures were explicitly interim.
- **Vodafone/JT/FICO:** readable page supports only its vendor-reported 30–40% fraud-loss reduction and up-to-55% false-positive reduction. The extra 30%/three-month and 44% figures need their actual case-study passage and denominator.
- **CISO local evidence for any operator result:** establish the exact workflow and stage; service/domain; identity and write authority; production/test boundaries; observation period; baseline/comparator; denominator; accepted-output rate; human rework/override; errors and harmful actions; rollback/fallback; service quality/availability; cost-to-run and supplier dependency. A productivity result alone is not security improvement or sector-wide ROI.

## 5. Pilot-draft gate

**Ready for a constrained pilot draft.** The six-chapter decision sequence can proceed without relying on the disputed Member State count, the unresolved Heavy Reading date relationship, weak operator performance percentages, or AI attribution in telecom intrusions. Chapter 2 should remain short and evidence-led; describe the Indonesian advisory as omitted/unverified, keep broader AI-misuse reporting explicitly cross-sector, and never infer AI use from intrusion sophistication or speed. Chapters 3–4 may use independent technical studies, guidance and clearly labelled local test questions; do not present unverified identity-to-network paths or response failures as observed operator practice. The public production example and differentiated pilot/deployment cases support a bounded account of current use, not prevalence, causal ROI or security efficacy. Operator interviews and local measurements remain important before making broad empirical claims or publication-level generalizations, but they are not a blocker to a pilot draft under these limits.
