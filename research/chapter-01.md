## Sources used

1. **Singapore CSA/IMDA, Operation CYBER GUARDIAN (9 February 2026).** Official disclosure reports a deliberate UNC3886 campaign targeting all four major Singapore telcos; a zero-day used to bypass a perimeter firewall in one instance; rootkits used for persistence/evasion in another; and a coordinated response spanning more than eleven months, with over 100 cyber defenders across government agencies working in partnership with the telcos. It says the actor reached parts of telco systems, including limited access to critical systems in one case, and exfiltrated a small amount of primarily network-related technical data. Authorities reported no evidence to date that sensitive or personal customer records were accessed or exfiltrated, or that services were disrupted. They also said defenders closed off UNC3886’s access points and expanded monitoring at the targeted telcos. The chapter distinguishes this confirmed technical-data exfiltration from the absence of evidence of customer-record access or service disruption. The chapter explicitly says this incident is not evidence of AI use. Source: https://www.csa.gov.sg/news-events/press-releases/largest-multi-agency-cyber-operation-mounted-to-counter-threat-posed-by-advanced-persistent-threat--apt--actor-unc3886-to-singapore-s-telecommunications-sector

2. **ENISA, NIS360 2026 (May 2026).** The EU agency assesses telecoms as among the most mature and critical NIS sectors. For digital infrastructure, it says legacy constraints and limited budgets hinder timely remediation, especially in telecommunications. It reports that 60% of surveyed companies in telecoms, core internet and trust services prioritise risks, define treatment plans and monitor progress; it separately notes uneven treatment practices in cloud/data-centre providers. Consistent response and recovery exercises remain an area of focus across digital infrastructure, particularly for high-impact scenarios. The chapter keeps these findings scoped to ENISA’s assessment and sample, not every operator. Source: https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-05/ENISA%20NIS360%202026.pdf

3. **CISA, ASD ACSC and partner agencies, “Careful Adoption of Agentic Artificial Intelligence (AI) Services” release (1 May 2026).** CISA’s directly retrieved release confirms the joint publication and names expanded attack surface, privilege creep, behavioral misalignment and obscure event records as risks. It recommends low-risk, non-sensitive use cases, avoiding broad access to sensitive data or critical systems, and including agentic AI security in the organization’s security model and risk posture. This is cross-sector guidance, not a telecom standard. The ASD ACSC landing page timed out on retrieval in this cycle; the chapter’s [3] maps to the accessible CISA release, not that landing page. Source: https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/cisa-us-and-international-partners-release-guide-secure-adoption-agentic-ai

4. **European Commission, NIS2 Directive policy page.** Used for background on NIS2 applying to electronic communications providers and the framework for cybersecurity risk management and incident reporting. This is policy context, not a new 2025–26 development. Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/nis2-directive
